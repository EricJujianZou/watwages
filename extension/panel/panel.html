How is ROI calculated?

ROI ranks each posting by how much pay you can expect for the effort of applying, given your chances of getting in.

Pay. The hourly pay in CAD, read from Compensation and Benefits. If a range is given, the middle of it is used. A posting with no stated pay gets the median pay of the postings on the board that do state it. Unpaid postings get 0.

Odds of getting in. Openings divided by the applications the posting is expected to have by its deadline. Every posting starts out assumed to have 80 applications per opening, since most co-op postings get between 80 and 500. As days pass, the real count takes over. Employer Student Direct doesn't show application counts, so those postings always use 80.

Level fit. Your level comes from your completed work terms: 0 or 1 is Junior, 2 or 3 is Intermediate, 4 or more is Senior. A posting open to your level gets 1. One level away gets 0.8 and two levels away gets 0.5.

Year requirement. If a posting asks for a higher year than you're in, it gets 0.15 when that year is required and 0.6 when it's only preferred. Otherwise it gets 1.

Repeat employer. You can only accept one offer, so an organization's second best posting gets 0.5 and each one after that gets 0.25.

Your weights. Each factor is raised to the power of its weight divided by 3. At 3 a factor counts exactly as described above, and at 5 it pulls about 1.7 times as hard. At 1 it counts a third as much.

Formula

value = paya × oddsb × level fitc × yeard × repeat employere

where a to e are each slider's weight ÷ 3

From value to ROI. Pay × odds gives your expected pay per application. ROI puts that on a fixed scale: 0 is minimum wage at 1 in 500, and 100 is $50/hr at 1 in 50. Each doubling of expected pay adds the same number of points. ROI doesn't compare postings with each other. If the best posting on a board scores 40, that board is weak and you may do better applying outside WaterlooWorks.

Note: company mission and prestige are not factored into ROI.
